Executive summary

Dogzie Town, in one page

Dogzie Town is a PlayStation console game set in the Dogzie Universe, an original IP created by Sania (known professionally as Valkyrie) in Québec. Players step into a town populated by dogs: they meet its residents, discover its buildings, and progress through a stylised open world, inspired by the luxury of Dubai and Saint-Martin, mixing exploration, high-speed pursuits and heist missions, all with a playful, humorous tone.

The universe already has meaningful creative depth — approximately 143 characters and 46 buildings designed for the project — and a clear creative identity, backed by the creator’s international art practice, the Valkyrie gallery. The game is in development and not yet complete. This dossier is not a launch announcement; it is an invitation to open a conversation.

01 · Concept & gameplay direction

A town where every dog has a story

Dogzie Town is a stylised open-world action game set in a town of dogs, with a look and feel inspired by the luxury of Dubai and Saint-Martin: sleek towers, sun, turquoise water and high-end living. Players live the life of the town, drive at full speed and take on missions, including story missions where factions meet (page 4). Progress is earned through play and held on the game server. No wallet, token or outside account is needed to play.

The gameplay mix is comparable to three well-known styles: an open world (GTA-like), speed and chases (Burnout-like), and team heist missions (Payday-like). All of it is told through Dogzie’s own cast and humour.

Open world

Steal cars, get tattoos, go shopping and buy a condo. A town full of things to do, places to own and ways to express yourself.

Comparable to: GTA

Speed & pursuits

Police chases you escape with signature moves, like sending the police car into a field or forcing it off the road. Each signature move earns bonus points.

Comparable to: Burnout

Heist missions

Planned missions: bank robberies, jewellery store heists and other heists, each one a set-piece challenge.

Comparable to: Payday

Luxury missions · Dubai & Saint-Martin

High-end set pieces in the town’s luxury setting: fight off sea pirates attacking yachts, run private jet missions, compete in plane tournaments, and take on other luxury challenges on the water and in the air.

About 143 characters designed, with seven collections planned to be playable. Everyone starts at the base level, so there is no pay-to-win (page 6). Dozens of buildings, shops and places to own, plus live events tied to the sports calendar (page 7).

“The sky is not the limit. It’s where Dogzie Town begins.”

Comparisons are for description only. Dogzie Town has no affiliation with Grand Theft Auto, Burnout, Payday or their publishers, and those names are trademarks of their owners. Detailed systems are still being finalised; there are no screenshots in this dossier. Given the car theft, pursuits and heists, the target age rating (ESRB/PEGI) would be set with a partner. The stylised dog characters and humorous tone would be kept, and the game would not be presented as a children’s title.

03 · What makes it distinctive

An original IP with depth from day one

Scale of the world

Around 143 characters and 46 buildings have been designed for the universe — a level of world-building that typically arrives only after several releases. It gives a partner room for content, seasons and spin-offs.

Console-first focus

Dogzie Town is conceived as a PlayStation console game: a living-room experience, made to be played on the couch, alone or with friends.

A single creative vision

The universe comes from one creator, Sania (Valkyrie), an international artist (see page 8). This gives it a coherent voice, a recognisable identity and a strong personal commitment to its future.

A brand with a signature

A distinctive black-and-gold identity, the paw emblem and the motto “The sky is not the limit” give Dogzie a premium, memorable presence beyond the game itself.

Fair by design · No pay-to-win

Core rule

Everyone starts equal. Every player begins with a base character, and characters cannot be bought already evolved. Progress is earned through play, not bought, so no one can pay to skip ahead.

Accessories are cosmetic and tradable. In the Dogzie Universe, players can trade or sell accessories to each other as NFTs. Style and self-expression can circulate, but power cannot be bought. In Dogzie Town on PlayStation, the same rule applies: progression is earned, no wallet is required, and any trading feature would only be added within PlayStation policies.

Universal appeal, room to grow

Dogs, cars, city life and humour work across markets. The Dogzie Universe continues to expand, and a partner can help shape its direction on PlayStation.

Character and building counts are taken from the project’s existing internal documentation and refer to designed assets for the universe; they are not a claim about shipped, in-game content.

04 · Why Sony & PlayStation

The natural home for Dogzie Town

Platform fit

Dogzie Town was conceived for PlayStation from the outset. A console-first, stylised action game with a large original cast fits PlayStation’s audience of open-world and action players.

Original IP with franchise potential

Dogzie is new, fully original and owned by the creator. With well over a hundred characters and dozens of locations already imagined, it offers room for updates, sequels or companion experiences, if the first game earns them.

What Sony’s expertise would bring

  • Production guidance, quality standards and certification know-how.
  • Development support and the tools to complete a PlayStation build.
  • Publishing, marketing and reach that an independent creator cannot achieve alone.

Long-term ecosystem vision

Dogzie could become a full ecosystem: console, blockchain, NFTs and its own token, Dogzy.

Seeking partner

Turning the universe into a finished PlayStation game takes a different scale of resources: a production team, console development and tooling, QA, certification, localisation, marketing and distribution. $500K–$550K+

What this shows a partner

  • A clear next step: Phase 2 is a defined production need with a budget target the creator has set.

A simple structure and a built-in launch plan

of the company is owned by the creator, Sania (Valkyrie). That keeps the structure simple for any publishing or partnership agreement.

5 × 1%

lifetime royalties planned for five influencers who would promote the game over the long term.

Influencer royalty programme

Players trust the creators they follow. Rather than paying for one-off promotion, the plan is to give each of five carefully chosen influencers a 1% lifetime royalty. Their interest then lines up with the game’s success over many years, not just at launch.

What each influencer commits to: mention Dogzie on every one of their live streams and share short updates on development progress. Over time this builds a community that is ready and waiting, so the game opens with a bang on launch day.

06 · What is needed next

From universe to finished console game

The following milestones describe the path to a complete PlayStation release. Timing and scope would be defined together with a partner.

Milestone 1 · Design lock

Finalise and document the core gameplay loop, progression and the selection of characters and buildings for the first release.

Milestone 2 · Vertical slice

A playable, representative portion of Dogzie Town demonstrating tone, controls and the core experience — the basis for a deeper partner evaluation.

Milestone 3 · Full production

Content build-out, game server and progression API, art and audio, accessibility and localisation (including French and English).

Milestone 4 · Platform readiness

Testing, quality assurance, PlayStation technical requirements and certification, age rating.

Milestone 5 · Launch & live care

Release on PlayStation, followed by updates that draw on the wider Dogzie universe.

Gaps we are looking to close

  • Production capacity and experienced console development support.
  • Platform tooling, technical guidance and certification know-how.
  • Publishing, marketing and distribution expertise.
